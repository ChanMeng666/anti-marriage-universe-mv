# 《战士阿花》 MV — Treatment

Style: Red Paper-cut (红色窗花剪纸). Length: the whole song, 239.52 s, 1920×1080, 24 fps.
Sound: the song is the entire soundtrack. No voice-over, no score, no foley. The picture locks to the song's beat grid and lyric timings. Every sung line is a burned-in banner subtitle and a line in `ahua.srt`.

## 1. Three candidate structures

**A. The window chronicle.** A lattice window of twelve panes; every lyric line pastes one window flower into a pane until the window is full, then the window opens. Tidy and very "paper-cut", but it is a slideshow: the heroine never acts, the three choruses would be three more panes, and the sarcasm of the song has nowhere to land.

**B. The un-making of a 囍.** The film starts on a finished wedding emblem, the double-happiness 囍, and spends four minutes taking it apart with scissors. Ah Hua is cut *out of* it in the first seconds; what the song throws away (the abacus, the script, the corset, the prince) is burned behind it; in the last bars the scissors run down the seam and 囍 becomes a single 喜, one happiness, hers. Between those bookends the story plays as a linear comedy in three ages (28 at the café, the short-haired traveller, 68 with her sisters), one age per chorus.

**C. One long road.** A single lateral tracking shot from the café to old age, everything met along the road. Strong for verse 2 and the bridge, but the café comedy needs shot/reverse-shot and close-ups, and four minutes of one move would be the "one even speed" failure.

**Chosen: B, with C's road borrowed for the instrumental bridge only.** B is the only one where the medium is the argument: wedding paper is literally what the song burns, scissors are both the craft and the weapon, and 囍 → 喜 is an ending nobody has to explain. It gives each chorus a different age and a different scale (one woman / one woman and a horse / a chain of sisters), so the same eight lines never replay the same footage.

## 2. Logline and arc

A woman cut from wedding paper refuses to be put back: she turns the blind date over, burns the bride's kit, walks out alone, and at sixty-eight cuts the double happiness down to a single one.

- **Setup (0–63 s):** scissors cut Ah Hua out of a 囍. Café blind date: Ah Fa (cut from teal paper, the only non-red figure) lectures, pulls out an abacus, an apron. She answers every prop with one small, precise move.
- **Turn (63–152 s):** she tips over the tower called tradition; the paper flips to night; the corset is tied into a bouquet and thrown into the fire basin a bride is supposed to step over. She cuts her hair, leaves alone, builds a life, takes the prince's horse.
- **Ending (152–239 s):** one long walk through the years to sixty-eight; the "home" unfolds into a fence and the fence is cut open; one figure unfolds into a chain of sisters (the native move, at the peak); the 囍 is cut in half; one 喜 stays lit.

One subject (Ah Hua), one goal (not to be folded back into the pattern), one turn (the tower falls, day paper flips to night paper).

## 3. Benchmark (my choice for this film)

- **Kara Walker's cut-paper tableaux** — learn: irony from the gap between a decorous, pretty craft and what the figures are actually doing; flat profile staging where every prop is a readable silhouette. Don't take: her figures, her subject matter, her compositions.
- **One-take lateral music videos in which the repeat changes each time round** (the Gondry school) — learn: a chorus is the same words with a larger frame each time; let a running gag (the prince) get shorter and funnier on each return. Don't take: any staging, set or trick shot.

## 4. Cast, paper families, props

- **Ah Hua** — China red `#d2201f`. Three cuts of the same rig: *28* (long hair with a flower, cheek rouge, long dress), *traveller* (short hair, jacket, trousers, satchel), *68* (hair in a bun cut almost to lace so it reads white, same trousers, a shawl). Faces: cold half-lid, smirk, sing/shout, serene.
- **Ah Fa** — teal dyed paper (`#1f7f86` family): the only figure not cut from wedding red, so he never belongs to her sheet. Suit, tie, pompadour. Faces: lecturing (mouth open), smug, scared. As the "prince" he is the same cut with a paper crown on a cream-white horse. A comic blowhard, never a threat: his defeats are pratfalls.
- **Sisters** (chorus 3) — the Ah Hua rig in pale red, China red and deep red with different hair, mirrored in a chain.
- **Tradition's things** (囍, tower, script, corset, veil, castle, fence) — one step deeper red (`#8f1218`), per STYLE §3 "an antagonist one step deeper".
- **Grounds** — day: warm rice paper `#efe2c6` with pale-red far layers. Night: a blue-black dyed family (`#141a2e` → `#232c4a`), only story pieces red. Ground and hero are always different families.
- **Gold foil** — once: the sun that rises on 「拥有了天下」, and it stays to the end.
- **Running props:** scissors (her sword), the fire basin (火盆), the 囍, the white horse, tear-off calendar leaves, the car key on the café table.

## 10. Not the demo again (checked after this treatment was written)

Compared with the style's demo (a 49 s New Year folktale) on the six points of DIRECTOR §11:

| Point | Demo | This film | |
|---|---|---|---|
| Structure | one linear tale: peace → threat → one craft act fixes the world | a 囍 taken apart over three ages, three choruses that each restage the same eight lines | differs |
| Opening | extreme close-up of scissors biting red paper, then the title | my first plan was also a scissor close-up: **it matched, so it was changed.** The film now opens flat on the finished 囍, whips to the scissors at work at medium distance, and pulls back to both | differs (after the change) |
| Signature shot | an eye filling a lattice window from inside a dark room | the bridge: one lateral walk in which every passing trunk takes years with it | differs |
| Camera path | pan a village → push to a window → pull back to many → pull back to a real window | café two-shots and close-ups → low wide → whip pan → gallop track → the long walk → rotation round a rosette → tilt up → ECU down a seam | differs |
| Score shape | original pentatonic chamber score, gong on the unfold | none: the user's neo-soul song, picture locked to its grid | differs |
| Ending | pull back: the film was a window flower; end card | the pair is cut, one half burns, a single 喜 stays lit while every other light goes out; no card | differs |

One device is shared on purpose and used differently: fold – unfold on a strong beat (STYLE §8). In the demo one cut lantern becomes eight; here one woman becomes a chain of eight who then each take their own face.

- Shots 34–35: in chorus 2 she does not ride off inside the prince shot; she is on the horse, rearing, by the end of 「滚回城堡吧」, and gallops in the next line.
- Shot 39: the second bouquet is tied with the tie she cut in verse 1 instead of carrying the veil and script.
- Shot 50: under the last calendar leaf there is a gold-foil leaf (the film's gold, continuing from the sun) rather than a bare backing.
- Shot 54: the three ages stand in front of the fire as silhouettes under the single 喜, with the sisters either side.
- Bracketed ad-libs: only the two that the word timings actually place ("Let it burn", "Coding my life") are shown, as small cream slips.
- Polish pass after the first build: in verse 2 the table lies face-on as a broad round top and Ah Fa ducks behind it with only his hair and eyes showing; chorus 3 has one prince, one roll, and a castle that shuts its door and folds closed like a card (it stays standing through the cut); after the bouquet, the date's props (mask, apron, the two paper men, the price tag) follow it into the fire on the quarter notes, and in the outro each sister feeds the fire in turn (apron, script, abacus, mask, pie, yesterday's leaf, a paper man, the tag) while flames and backlight pulse on the quarter notes; in the intro her face, hair and dress pattern come up behind the blades as they travel, and the offcut falls away around her.
