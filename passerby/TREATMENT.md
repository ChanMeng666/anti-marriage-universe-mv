# 《最佳路人》 The Best Strangers — treatment

Music video, 354.80 s, 1920×1080, 24 fps. Style: Risograph Print. The song is the whole soundtrack.

## 1. Three candidate structures

**A. Two inks in dialogue.** Shots alternate with the singer: his shots in teal, hers in red, both only in the choruses. Clean and obvious, but it is the first idea the style offers, and over six minutes it becomes a tennis match: the picture would only illustrate who has the microphone.

**B. A fixed diptych.** The frame is split by a gutter for the whole film, red world left, teal world right, the café table straddling the gutter. Strong for the chorus ("平行的宇宙"), but a locked layout for 355 s has no turn: the date and the afterlife would look the same.

**C. One print job (chosen).** The film is the make-ready and run of a single sheet. Two plates are being registered against each other, and the whole comedy is that they refuse to register:
1. *Make-ready* (intro): two registration crosses, one red, one teal, slide towards each other on bare paper and slip past on the finger snaps.
2. *His proof, her proof* (verse 1): the café date is pulled twice, once per plate. In his proof (teal only) he is solid and she is a blank knockout in her chair; in her proof (red only) she is solid and he is the blank, which is how he becomes a clay statue from an old dynasty.
3. *Both plates down* (pre-chorus): the two proofs land on one sheet, visibly off register; on "双向欺诈" the registration snaps true for the only time in the film and the table where the plates overlap prints the third colour.
4. *The plates slide apart* (chorus 1): a diptych made by pulling the sheet in two: she codes, he rides.
5. *Hearsay in halftone* (verse 2): what each has only *heard* about the other is printed as a tint, never a solid: his teal voice over her short-haired life printed in dots of red, and the reverse.
6. *Masks are knockouts* (pre-chorus 2): two masks made of missing ink; the plates arrive underneath and the faces are the same shape.
7. *The map* (chorus 2): top-down, two routes on one city plan that run parallel and never cross; the hypothetical shared roof is the only place where all three plates are halftoned together, and it prints as mud: 豆腐渣.
8. *Into the dots* (the four-second break before chorus 2): the halftone screen itself opens up until the dots are discs. *The bridge*: two messages across a keyboard that plays itself, a ring he rides past, a sofa as long as a limousine, and the toast that does not clink.
9. *The finished sheet* (outro): pull back, it was one sheet; it is trimmed down the middle into two prints that leave by opposite edges, and the two crosses of the opening are left side by side.

Why C: it gives the "perfect miss" a physical mechanism (registration), it gives each song section its own print logic so six minutes keep changing, and it ends where it began without replaying anything.

## 2. Logline and arc

Two people who each sabotaged the same blind date find out, a plate at a time, that the other was doing it too, and toast the misprint that saved them both.

- Setup: one café table printed twice, each proof with a hole where the other person should be.
- Turn: both plates on one sheet, the one in-register moment ("双向欺诈"): they see each other, and choose to stay off register.
- Ending: one sheet, two prints that never touch; the registration crosses from the opening sit side by side, at peace.

Native move at the peak: **two inks, a third where they meet**, the café table as the only full red-over-teal overprint, on the snap into register. Its dark twin is the three-plate mud of "同一个屋檐下" in chorus 2.

## 3. Inks and what they mean

| Plate | Ink | Carries |
|---|---|---|
| R | Red `#F04E4A` | 阿花, and everything that is hers |
| G | Teal `#00838A` | 阿发, and everything that is his |
| B | Sunflower `#FFB511` | everyone else: the café light, the matchmaker, the roof, "圆满"; used sparingly, as a light tint for skin and as the sheet's warm third |

Red over teal prints a near-black plum: the colour of the only thing they ever shared, the table. Paper `#F4EEE2`. No area takes more than two plates, one of them a tint, except the deliberate mud of the shared roof.

## 4. Benchmark

- **Mid-century two-colour jazz record sleeves** (the tradition of duotone photographs under heavy grotesque type). Learn: type as a picture element, one colour block and a lot of air, the syncopated placement of small things against a big flat field. Do not take: any sleeve's layout, photograph, typeface or name.
- **The split-screen conversations of 1970s New York romantic comedy** (two people in two rooms answering each other across the frame line). Learn: parallel staging as comedy, the cut withheld so both reactions play at once, dry delivery. Do not take: characters, rooms, lines, shots.

## 5. Characters (flat silhouettes, no outlines, paper halo)

- **阿花 (red).** At the date: long hair to the shoulder blades, a dress, sitting very straight, hand on her cup, scalp crawling. Later: a short bob, a hoodie, a laptop, bare feet on a chair. Acting: small, precise, withering; a slow head turn is her biggest move.
- **阿发 (teal).** At the date: slick quiff, wide-shouldered suit, tie, leaning back with his arm over the chair, the key slapped on the table. Later: helmet, jacket, a low motorcycle, a long road. Acting: broad on the date (he is overplaying), loose and small afterwards.
- **The matchmaker** never appears: only a yellow speech shape that enters from the frame edge.
- Props: a round café table, two cups, the key (a plain key with a blank fob), a milk-tea cup that is conspicuously absent, a laptop, a motorcycle (generic, no badge), two masks, two glasses.

## 7. Sound

The user's song is the entire soundtrack: no voice-over, no score, no foley are added. The directing rules on sound become rules on listening:

| Song passage | What the picture does with it |
|---|---|
| Piano and bass intro | bare paper, two marks, slow drift |
| Finger snaps | registration kicks, one per snap |
| Verses (conversational) | long held compositions, character acting on twos, cuts on bar lines only |
| Pre-chorus lift | plates gate in and out on the half-bar |
| Chorus | widest frames, tracking, the fastest cutting of the film |
| Break after chorus 1 | held frame with a slow registration drift, snapping back on the next downbeat |
| Piano solo bridge | the long take (push into dots) |
| Fade-out | ink running out |

The song's own breaks are the film's silences. Audio is muxed untouched apart from loudness normalisation to −14 LUFS.

## 9. Subtitles and titles

- Every sung line is burned in: a paper-white knockout strip at the bottom, text on **one plate**: teal when 阿发 sings, red when 阿花 sings, and a **double-hit** (teal and red, offset a few pixels) when both sing. The strip arrives on a cut and leaves on a cut; no fades.
- Spoken lines use the same strip, in quotation marks. Ad-libs, where audible, appear small in the margin.
- Title 最佳路人: heavy grotesque, double-hit in both inks, on the intro's snaps. Chinese: Noto Sans SC 900/700 (subset). Latin: Jost 600–700.
- Words that belong to the picture (工作稳定, 凯迪拉克, 豆腐渣, 路人甲) are set as printed type inside the scene, on a plate.

## 10. What changed once the song was measured

- The lyric sheet promises a piano solo in the bridge. In the recording the bridge is short: the spoken exchange starts 1.3 s after chorus 2 ends (310.3 s) and the sung bridge follows at once. The long take planned for the solo became the four-second break before chorus 2 (244.8 s), where the song really does stop to breathe.
- The intro's spoken lines sit at 9.0–11.9 s and 12.6–14.2 s, right against the first downbeat, so the two speech slabs stay up over the cut into verse 1 (the teal plate rolls on underneath them).
- The music ends at about 347.5 s and the file is silent from about 351 s: the last image (the two crosses and the title) is held through the silence.
- The ad-libs of the outro ("Bye bye… Good luck…") could not be found in the recording and are not shown.

## Appendix: cue map

The low hits fall on every eighth tracked beat starting at beat 6 (3.95 s), which is where the bar lines are taken; verse 1 comes in on the downbeat at 14.23 s.
